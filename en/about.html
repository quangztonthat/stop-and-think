Why Stop & Think exists

Most books don't need re-reading.

Most non-fiction is a 30-page essay stretched into 300 pages. One good idea, one example, repeated nine different ways. You finish it, remember 1-2 sentences, then forget.

A few books are different. They force you to pause. As you read, you stop turning pages — you close the book, look out the window, and ask: "When did I start getting this wrong?"

That's the kind of book Stop & Think writes about.

This library isn't a place to summarize books. It's where I write down what books changed about how I see — work, money, family, myself.

I read 1-2 books a week. The ones worth pausing for, I write back — not as a summary, but as a distillation: core idea, clarifying example, questions to apply. Over time, this library becomes a place I return to — not just to remember books I've read, but to see how I've changed after each one.

Who I write for

People who've read enough to doubt books.

Stop & Think isn't for beginning readers. I don't summarize self-help into 7 shareable tips. Plenty of channels do that better than me.

I write for people who've read some books and started realizing: not every book is worth reading, not every idea is worth remembering. For people who want to filter — and filter carefully.
