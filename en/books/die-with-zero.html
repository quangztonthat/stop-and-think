Die with Zero.

The goal of life isn't to die with the most money — it's to die with the most fulfillment. Saving = postponing experience to an age you can no longer enjoy it. Perkins (ex-Wall Street trader) lays out 9 rules that break the "save more, spend later" mantra. Memory dividends, time-buckets, peak net worth ~45-60 (not 65), give while alive. Counter for VN context: filial piety + emergency buffer + generational wealth gap.

Central thesis

Saving until death = postponing experience until you can't enjoy it. The edge of a good life = converting money into memory while you still can.

Perkins writes Die with Zero as an argument book — not a story, but a defense of 9 rules, each challenging society's "save more, spend later" mantra. Background: Perkins is an ex-Wall Street trader (energy hedge fund) who made money early and big, so he's earned the right to talk about post-money lessons. Core idea: money is the instrument, fulfillment is the goal. Excess saving = wasted life energy.

3 main frameworks: Memory Dividend (experiences compound through replay; money does too — but differently); Time-Bucket (divide your life into decades, each decade has experiences that fit it — at 60 you can't climb Everest even with $5M); Net Worth Curve (peak net worth is NOT at 65 retirement but ~45-60, then decumulate). Plus: Inheritance Timing (give while alive — kids need money at 25-35, not 60); Health-Wealth-Time Triangle (you never have all 3 at once). I applied this partially: 2024 trip to Japan with family, spent 2x normal budget. Memory dividend was huge — but you can never truly "die with zero" in the VN context.

"
Maximizing fulfillment, not wealth, should be your life's goal.
Bill Perkins, Die with Zero

Perkins writes for US/UK upper-middle-class with 401(k) + Medicare + nuclear family — VN reality differs on 3 axes

Perkins assumes you have a social safety net (401k, Medicare, baseline Social Security), a nuclear family (you support 2-3 people), and generational wealth that's already formed (parents are settled, don't need your support). Apply the "die with zero" formula to a Vietnamese family of 8 → reckless. Gen 1 in VN who become wealthy must build wealth to pass to gen 2 — because gen 1 inherited nothing, and if gen 2 inherits, they start 25 years ahead. "Die with zero" in the VN context = restarting the poverty cycle for the next generation.

3 axes where VN differs from Perkins's assumptions: (1) Filial piety — money is a responsibility for parents/grandparents/extended family, not just yourself; (2) Emergency buffer reality — public hospitals are overloaded, severe illness needs $50-200K cash flow now, public health insurance covers only 30-50%; (3) Generational wealth gap — gen 1 cannot "consume to zero" because gen 2 needs seed capital. Perkins's framework needs adapting: "die with just enough" instead of zero. Just enough = (parents care reserve) + (family emergency fund) + (kids seed capital ~25-35).

Skin in game 2024 trip to Japan with family — spent 2x my normal budget for a 10-day trip with my 70-year-old father in Kyoto and my 65-year-old mother in Hokkaido. Memory dividend so high that my dad still tells the stories a year later. Perkins was right here. BUT: 2025 my mother fell suddenly ill, hospital bills + specialist treatment cost $35K in Q1. If I had truly "died with zero" Perkins-style → no money to save my mother. The reserve fund ($X stable) saved a life. Q3/2024 I advised an employee, 28 years old, to spend $20K solo in Europe — exact time-bucket logic: he's 35 now with a family, will never do it again. Apply selectively for high-memory-dividend experiences, but never on top of the emergency buffer.
Framework 01 / Memory Compound

$1000 invested vs $1000 in experience — which line really compounds?

Perkins chapter 2: "Investing in experiences pays dividends — those dividends are memories." $1000 invested in stocks compounds in dollars (8%/year). But $1000 spent on a trip at 25 creates a memory replayed 1000+ times across the next 60 years — each replay = 1 unit of fulfillment. Total fulfillment compound exceeds monetary ROI. But memory only replays if you're still alive and still remember — investment timing matters.

Insight
Optimal allocation
Perkins isn't saying "don't save". He says: balance the experience curve against the save curve. 20-40 tilt toward EXPERIENCE (capacity high). 40-60 tilt toward SAVE (capacity falling). 60+ DECUMULATE.
Framework 02 / Time Buckets

6 decade-buckets — each decade has an experience window that can't move to another decade

Perkins chapter 7: "Think of your life as distinct seasons." Divide life into decade buckets. Each bucket holds experiences that fit your physical/family/career capacity in that decade. Window not transferable: at 60 you can't climb Everest even with $5M. At 70 you can't hold a newborn grandchild even with all the time in the world. At 25 you can solo-backpack 6 months — once you have a family + career + mortgage, you can't anymore. Color decreases = health/capacity declines.

20s · 21-30
Discovery
Solo backpack 6 months. Risky career bet. Late nights. Extreme sport. No dependents, no mortgage. Unique window — after 30 you can't do this kind of thing.
100% capacity
30s · 31-40
Building
Skill compound. Career inflection. Marriage + first kid. Hardest physical years raising young children. Money rises but time crashes. Window: build the foundation for the 40s peak.
92% capacity
40s · 41-50
Peak
Income peak for many. Kids 5-15. Family travel window — kids old enough to travel, young enough to enjoy. Skiing + diving + hiking moderate. Don't postpone — the 50s are different.
80% capacity
50s · 51-60
Pivot
Net worth peak ~55. Kids leave home. Begin decumulation — Perkins's most important rule. Cardio declines 10-15%. Joints fail. Last decade for a 14-day Annapurna trek.
65% capacity
60s · 61-70
Harvest
Knee/hip surgery common 65+. Cruise > backpack. Grandchildren window — physical bonding with young grandkids. Decumulate aggressively: give kids money NOW (down payment, kids), don't make them inherit at 80.
45% capacity
70s+ · 71+
Wisdom
Cognitive sharpness still high (curve well into 80s). Travel slowly. Legacy window — write, mentor, give. Physical experience window CLOSED. Money utility ~0 at 80+ — your peak spending is past.
25% capacity

Apply: list 3 experiences in your current bucket that you're postponing to a later bucket. Postpone = throw away. The later bucket won't support it. Personal: at 38 I've skipped a Patagonia trek twice with "wait until after 40 when the kid is older". Reality: in the 40s the kid hits school years → harder to leave. Rescheduled to 39 in Q4/2026.

Framework 03 / Triangle Tradeoff

Health-Wealth-Time — you never have all 3 at once

Perkins chapter 6: 3 life resources — Health (physical capacity), Wealth (financial resources), Time (free time to use). At any life stage you only own 2 of 3 corners. Student: time + health, no wealth. Mid-career: wealth + health, no time. Retiree: wealth + time, no health. Solution: convert from your strong corners to the weak one — use wealth to buy time (outsource), use time to invest in health (gym/sleep), use health to create wealth (productive work).

Stage 1 · 20s-30s
Young
HAVE: Health + Time
MISS: Wealth
Convert: time + health → wealth (work hard, build skill, take career risk). Don't waste time on low-leverage activities. Cheap distant trips OK — you don't need a $5K hotel at 25. Backpacking $30/day equals the memory of $500/day luxury.
Stage 2 · 40s-50s
Mid-career
HAVE: Wealth + Health
MISS: Time
Convert: wealth → time (outsource cleaning, cooking, admin). $200/month cleaner = 16h/month = 192h/year = a full month back. Don't penny-pinch on time savers. Family travel is mandatory — the health window is closing.
Stage 3 · 60s+
Retired
HAVE: Wealth + Time
MISS: Health
Convert: wealth → health (best PT, nutritionist, sleep clinic, early joint surgery). Don't hoard wealth — utility past ~80 = 0. Decumulate aggressively: kids need money at 30-40, not 60. Give while alive.
Perkins's 9 rules — grouped into 3 parts

Map of Die with Zero

Perkins writes 9 chapters = 9 rules. I condense them into 3 PARTS by practical arc: Foundation (rules 1-3, defending the thesis for "die with zero"); Engineering (rules 4-6, tools + mechanisms — annuities, give while alive, autopilot off); Calibration (rules 7-9, time-bucketing + peak detection + risk asymmetry).

PART 1 · Foundation — Rules 1-3
Why spend earlier, not save later — defending the thesis
Maximize experience. Invest early. Aim for zero.
RULE 01
Maximize positive life experiences
Opens with Erin & John story: skipped a $400 dance show "to save". 5 years later John dies suddenly — Erin regrets forever. Perkins's life energy framework: experience = the real currency; money is just a proxy. The Ant vs Grasshopper fable: "when does the ant ever get to play?"
Experience as currency
RULE 02
Start investing in experiences early
Memory dividend compounds. $1000 trip @25 generates 60 years of replay = compound fulfillment. Lifetime fulfillment graph: integrate area under (experience × replay years). Maximize area = front-load experience while capacity (health + free time + curiosity) is highest.
Memory dividend
RULE 03
Aim to die with zero
John Arnold story: $4B by 38, still couldn't spend it down. "Brewster's Millions problem". Excess money = wasted life energy (hours of work converted to nothing). Warning about autopilot: the savings habit doesn't stop on its own; you must actively decumulate.
Decumulate
PART 2 · Engineering — Rules 4-6
Mechanisms + tools to die with zero (safely)
Annuities. Give while alive. Off autopilot.
RULE 04
Use all available tools
Fear of dying with $0 comes from longevity risk (living longer than projected). Perkins's solution: annuities — convert lump sum to monthly income for life. Insurance company bears the longevity risk. Frees you mentally to spend down the rest. VN: annuity market not yet mature → adapt differently.
Annuity
RULE 05
Give money when it has most impact
Median age of inheritance in the US = 60. Kids at 60 are settled — money utility low. Optimal give-age 25-35: down payment, kids' early years, business capital. Perkins's exhortation: separate "kid's gift" from estate. "Don't make your kids wait until you die."
Inheritance timing
RULE 06
Don't live on autopilot
Save autopilot is easy → wasteful. Spend autopilot is easy → wasteful. Perkins: actively design your budget by life-stage. 30s: peak save 25-30% of income. 50s: shift to spend 70%+ on experience. 70s: pure decumulation. Override the default 401k mindset.
Active design
PART 3 · Calibration — Rules 7-9
Time-bucketing + peak detection + asymmetric risk
Distinct seasons. Know your peak. Take risks young.
RULE 07
Think life as distinct seasons
Time-buckets framework. Each decade has experiences that don't transfer. List your bucket list — assign each item to the decade that fits its physical/family capacity. Schedule reverse-engineered: "at 60 you can't climb Everest even with $5M."
Time bucket
RULE 08
Know your peak (net worth)
Conventional: peak net worth = retirement age 65. Reality: optimal peak ~45-60 years old (rule of thumb: the age at which saved x ≥ annual spend × remaining years × safety margin). After peak: actively decumulate. Most people peak at death = $1M+ wasted. Perkins's calculator: the peak point.
Peak NW
RULE 09
Take biggest risks young
Asymmetric risk math: failing at 25 = 40 years to recover. Failing at 55 = 10 years. Risk capacity DECLINES with age. Young: take bold career bets, geographic moves, business risk. Don't wait "until safer" — safer is a proxy for older = less time to recover.
Asymmetric
