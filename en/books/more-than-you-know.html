PROCESS
Central thesis

Investing is a multidisciplinary art — not just finance. Wisdom accumulates across many lenses: psychology, biology, network theory, complex systems.

Mauboussin wrote More Than You Know as an essay collection — 38 essays grouped into 4 themes: Investment Philosophy (process vs outcome, attribute vs circumstance), Psychology (Babe Ruth effect, multiple promotions, emotion), Innovation & Strategy (creative destruction, network effects, business cycle), Science & Complexity (phase transitions, mean reversion vs persistence, fitness landscape). Background: Mauboussin spent 25+ years as Credit Suisse Chief Investment Strategist + Columbia Business School professor. Signature move: borrow lenses from outside finance to solve finance problems.

3 core frameworks: Process vs Outcome (4 quadrants — judging an investor by outcome alone is misleading because luck dominates short-term); Babe Ruth Effect (frequency wrong + magnitude right = winning formula; aim for slugging not batting average); Multiple Time Horizons (different participants see different markets — edge comes from identifying which horizon you compete on). Plus: Complexity & Phase Transitions (markets behave physically — quiet → quiet → SUDDEN; iceberg metaphor) and Mean Reversion vs Persistence (financial returns mean-revert, operational performance can persist with moats). I applied this partially: a Q3/2024 acquisition deal — built a process checklist, scored 7/10, passed → an insider then took it at 6/10 → 3x in 18 months. Process is a valid framework, but in VN short-term outcome luck dominates.

"
The reasoning that goes into a decision is more important than the result of that decision.
Michael Mauboussin, More Than You Know

Mauboussin's reference base = US large-cap public markets with thick analyst coverage and decades of data — VN reality is the opposite, signal-to-noise is weak

Mauboussin writes for an audience with process auditability: standardized SEC filings, public analyst notes, free conference call transcripts, Bloomberg terminal showing flow. "Process focus over outcome" assumes you have data and tools to audit a fund manager's process — 10+ year track record, factor decomposition, behavioral patterns. VN reality is the opposite: SME deals are mostly private, no analyst coverage, the founder tells one story to the bank, another to the investor, a third to the spouse. Mauboussin's framework assumes process is visible — in VN, only outcome is visible to outsiders, process is invisible.

3 axes where VN diverges from what Mauboussin assumes: (1) Signal-to-noise ratio is weak — short-term outcome luck dominates 70-80%; you need 5-10 years of process discipline before edge becomes visible. Most VN retail investors don't have that patience. (2) Sample size is insufficient — VNI is only 25 years old, with fewer than 50 full cycles; Mauboussin uses 100+ years of US data to argue mean reversion. VN doesn't yet have enough data to confirm patterns. (3) Multiple time horizons is hard to apply — Mauboussin assumes HFT/swing/long-term/multi-decade actors all present, edge from identifying which horizon. VN is 80% retail-dominated, mostly swing traders on a 2-6 month horizon — multi-decade actors (foreign funds, insurance companies) are few. Adapt: borrow the framework (process discipline, Babe Ruth slugging, complexity awareness) — but accept that luck-noise dominates short-term. Track decisions in a journal for 3-5 years before judging skill.

Skin in game Q3/2024 SME acquisition pass — I built a 3-page process checklist (industry tailwind, team quality, balance sheet, valuation, exit option), scored the deal 7/10 → passed because of one red axis. An insider then took the deal at a 6/10 process score (I asked them later, they confirmed), made 3x in 18 months thanks to early consumer recovery. Mauboussin lens: process is a valid framework — don't abandon it. Outcome: one decision = sample of 1 = no signal on process. You need 20+ decisions before signal emerges. My personal stock pick in 2023 with VNM: bought on narrative (oat milk launch hype), no process — bad process, bad outcome (lawsuit + revenue miss → -22%). Lesson: process saves you next time, outcome doesn't. 2022 VN real estate short: built a clean leverage analysis process (debt/EBITDA 6x+ across many firms) — process right, but timing wrong (squeeze for 6 months), exited -18%. Babe Ruth lens: didn't size for survival — shorting with personal leverage = liquidated quickly. Lesson: process and sizing must combine — process right + size wrong = blown up before the thesis is valid.
Framework 01 / Process vs Outcome

2x2 matrix — judge by process, not outcome

Mauboussin Essay 2: "In an activity where luck plays a meaningful role, the quality of an outcome is a poor indicator of the quality of the process." Investing fits this exactly: 4 quadrants — good process + good outcome (skill); good process + bad outcome (bad luck); bad process + good outcome (dumb luck); bad process + bad outcome (just deserts). Most people judge by outcome only → end up rewarding dumb luck and punishing bad luck → wrong incentives. Mauboussin's edge: judge by process, accept short-term outcome variance.

Quadrant 1 · Skill
Good process + Good outcome
Repeatable. Buffett over 50 years, Soros breaking the pound, Mauboussin as a process-driven analyst. Action: keep doing. Document the process — replicate. This is the quadrant everyone wants — but rare short-term because of luck noise.
Quadrant 2 · Bad Luck
Good process + Bad outcome
Process was right but a tail event happened. The 2022 BĐS short leverage analysis was right — a 6-month squeeze killed the position. Action: keep process — repeat. Don't change strategy because of one bad outcome. Sample size 1 = noise.
Quadrant 3 · Dumb Luck
Bad process + Good outcome
A YOLO meme stock 2021 +200% — gambler wins once. SPAC retail flips. Action: don't repeat. A lucky outcome teaches the wrong lesson — reinforces a bad habit. Most dangerous quadrant — feels like skill but isn't.
Quadrant 4 · Just Deserts
Bad process + Bad outcome
FOMO crypto top 2022. Trusting a friend's tip without research. Action: change process. The outcome confirms the process is bad. Easiest quadrant to learn from — pain forces change. Painful but useful signal.
Framework 02 / Babe Ruth Effect

Frequency wrong + magnitude right — aim for slugging, not batting average

Mauboussin chapter 16: "In expected value terms, frequency does not matter — magnitude does." Babe Ruth: 1923 highest strikeout rate in the same year as highest home run rate. Lesson for investing: VC and value investing both share the high % miss + few outliers cover all pattern. Sequoia Capital portfolio: 80% of deals underperform/zero, 5% of deals cover 90% of returns. Don't aim for batting average (high % win) — aim for slugging (magnitude when right). Asymmetric payoff distributions change everything about sizing and holding strategy.

80 / 100 decisions
-25%
Misses. Average loss small (capped via position sizing). Batting average .200 = lose 80% of the time. Painful psychologically — feels like failing constantly.
15 / 100 decisions
+2x
Singles. Modest wins 1.5-3x. Total contribution ~15% of returns. Nice-to-have but doesn't change the game. Most retail aim here — wrong target.
5 / 100 decisions
+30x
Home runs. 10x-100x outliers. Cover 90%+ of total returns. This is alpha. Sequoia: 5% of deals carry the portfolio. Apply: position sizing so home runs have room to compound (hold long, don't cap upside early).
Framework 03 / Multiple Time Horizons

4 horizons — different actors, different signals, different games

Mauboussin Essay 9: "Markets are made up of many participants with very different time horizons. The 'price' you see is the integration of all these players." Edge = identify which horizon you compete on, know that other actors are at different horizons watching different things. HFT (microseconds) reads order flow. Swing traders (weeks) read momentum. Long-term (years) reads fundamentals. Multi-decade (10+ yrs) reads regime change. Mismatching horizons = competing in a losing game. VN retail typically swings 2-6 months but uses multi-decade signals (DCF intrinsic value) → mismatch → frustrated.

HFT
microseconds · μs
Actors: Citadel, Virtu, Renaissance Tech. Co-located servers. Fight for $0.0001 spread × millions of trades/day.
Signal: order flow imbalance, latency arbitrage. Fundamental data IRRELEVANT — too slow. Game retail can't enter.
Swing
days to weeks · 1d-3m
Actors: retail traders, momentum funds, social media followers (FB groups VN, Telegram pumps). 80% of VN volume is here.
Signal: chart patterns, news flow, sentiment shifts. Fundamentals MATTER LITTLE — narrative drives. Crowded — edge is hard.
Long-term
years · 1-10y
Actors: value funds, family offices, patient retail. Buffett's "best holding period is forever". 10-15% of VN volume.
Signal: intrinsic value DCF, ROIC trend, moat durability. Fundamentals DOMINATE. Less crowded — edge is possible for patient retail.
Multi-decade
10+ years · regime
Actors: sovereign wealth, insurance companies, generational wealth (Walton, Mars). 5% of VN volume — foreign funds + insurance + a few families.
Signal: demographic shifts, regulatory regime, geopolitics, technology super-cycles. Annual fundamentals IRRELEVANT — read the 50-year arc. Least crowded — biggest edge.

Apply: identify one horizon you compete on + accept you don't compete in others. Personally: I run 5% swing, 60% long-term, 35% multi-decade. Skip HFT (no infrastructure) + skip swing FOMO (crowded). Edge: long-term + multi-decade are niches with less competition. Applied Q3/2024: passed an SME deal because I'm a long-term thinker (consumer recovery 6 months) — long-term horizon needs a 5+ year leverage runway, the deal had 4x leverage so it didn't pass. The insider took it (swing thinker) — short-term right outcome.

38 essays — condensed 4 parts

Map of More Than You Know

Mauboussin wrote 38 essays grouped into 4 themes. Each theme has ~10 essays — borrowing lenses cross-domain (psychology, biology, complex systems, network theory) to solve finance problems. I've condensed them into 4 PARTS: Investment Philosophy (process, attribute vs circumstance, second-order); Psychology (Babe Ruth, multiple promotions, emotion); Innovation & Strategy (creative destruction, network effects, business cycle); Science & Complexity (phase transitions, mean reversion vs persistence, fitness landscape).

PART 1 · Investment Philosophy — Essays 1-10
Process over outcome. Attribute vs circumstance. Position sizing.
Foundation: how to think about investing as discipline, not gambling.
ESSAY 01
Process vs Outcome
In activities with high luck → outcome is a poor indicator of process quality. Judge process, not result. 4 quadrants: skill, bad luck, dumb luck, just deserts. Most evaluators look only at outcomes → reward dumb luck, punish bad luck. Wrong incentive structure.
Process matters
ESSAY 02
Be the House (Position Sizing)
Casinos don't bet against single players — they bet on millions of trials with edge. Investors: size positions to weather worst case, not bet on single conviction. Kelly criterion sizing: f = (bp - q) / b. Most retail over-size winners → ruin.
Sizing > conviction
ESSAY 03
Risk & Uncertainty (Knight)
Knight 1921: risk = probabilities knowable (coin flip). Uncertainty = probabilities unknowable (next pandemic). Markets are mostly uncertainty, not risk. Stop using Gaussian models for fat-tailed reality. Plan for ranges, not point estimates.
Knight distinction
ESSAY 04
Are You an Expert?
Tetlock's 30-year study: experts no better than chance at long-term forecasting. Hedgehog (one big idea) is worse than fox (many small models). Investing: be a fox — hold multiple lenses, update on evidence.
Be fox not hedgehog
ESSAY 05
Right from the Get-Go
Anchoring trap: the first number you see frames the entire analysis. Real estate: agent's ask price is the anchor. Stock: IPO price is the anchor. Calculate intrinsic FIRST, look at price SECOND. Reverse the order to defeat the anchor.
Anchor defense
ESSAY 06
Survival of the Fittest
Survivorship bias kills analysis. Mutual fund 30-year track records only show survivors — failed funds are delisted. "3% beat the market" claim ignores 70% that closed. Always ask: what's missing from my data?
Survivorship bias
ESSAY 07
Outcome Bias
Judging a decision by outcome, not process at the time. The same decision can have different outcomes — luck. Pre-commit decision criteria before knowing the outcome. Review process quarterly — was the reasoning sound regardless of result?
Pre-commit criteria
ESSAY 08
Time & Patience
Compound interest = the 8th wonder, said Einstein. Time horizon = competitive advantage. Most investors have a 12-month focus → opportunity for a 10-year focus. Buffett: "time is the friend of a wonderful business, the enemy of a mediocre one".
Time arbitrage
ESSAY 09
Multiple Time Horizons
Different participants on different horizons → different "markets" at the same price. Edge = identify your horizon + accept you don't compete in others. HFT vs swing vs long vs multi-decade — pick a lane. VN retail is mostly swing — crowded.
Pick horizon
ESSAY 10
Capital Allocation = CEO's Job
"In a properly run business, capital allocation decisions are the chairman's most important job." 5 channels: reinvest, M&A, dividend, buyback, debt repay. Most CEOs are bad at this — promoted from operations, not finance. Look for capital-allocator CEOs.
CEO allocation
PART 2 · Psychology of Investing — Essays 11-19
Babe Ruth effect. Multiple promotions. Emotion management.
How the brain breaks during investing — and how to compensate.
ESSAY 11
Babe Ruth Effect
Ruth 1923: highest strikeout rate + highest home runs in the same year. Lesson: frequency wrong + magnitude right = winning. Sequoia: 5% of deals cover 90% of returns. Aim for slugging not batting average. Position size so home runs can compound — don't cap upside early.
Slugging
ESSAY 12
Sorting Sheep from Goats
Manager evaluation: how to distinguish skill from luck? Mauboussin's test: process consistency + diverse outcomes. Skill = stable process across regimes. Luck = lucky in one regime, fails in the next. Need 5+ years of data across multiple regimes.
Skill test
ESSAY 13
Right Tail of the Distribution
Most investing returns are concentrated in a few outliers. Right tail = where money is made. Strategy: many small bets to get exposure to the right tail, sized so the left tail (losers) don't kill you. Asymmetric payoff design.
Right tail focus
ESSAY 14
Multiple Promotions Bias
Why do 80% of mutual funds underperform? Career risk: managers are promoted on short-term results → forced index hugging → can't outperform. Independent capital (founders, family offices, retail) has a structural advantage — no career risk.
Career risk trap
ESSAY 15
Information Cascades
When everyone defers to "what others know" — an information cascade forms. Stock bubbles work this way. Independent thinkers break the cascade. Pre-commit your position before reading the consensus. Read consensus AFTER deciding.
Independent thinking
ESSAY 16
Emotion & Decision
Damasio neuroscience: emotion is BUILT INTO decision-making — patients with damaged emotion brains can't decide. Goal: don't eliminate emotion — manage it. Pre-commit rules so emotion can't override at the moment of stress.
Manage emotion
ESSAY 17
Stress & Performance
Inverted-U: low stress = under-perform, medium = peak, high = collapse. Most investors operate in the high-stress zone (leverage, concentrated, daily monitoring). Reduce stress to medium → better decisions. Cash buffer + position rules → reduce stress.
Inverted-U
ESSAY 18
Wisdom of Crowds (and Folly)
Galton 1906 ox weight: crowd average is accurate. WHEN: independent + diverse + decentralized. WHEN crowds fail: information cascades + herding → wrong consensus. Markets oscillate between wisdom and folly. Read which mode you're in.
Wisdom vs folly
ESSAY 19
Mind on Money
Why do so many smart people fail at investing? Smart in one domain ≠ smart across domains. A doctor good at medicine = bad at investing (different feedback loops, different uncertainty). Domain expertise is non-transferable. Stay humble outside your circle of competence.
Domain humility
PART 3 · Innovation & Competitive Strategy — Essays 20-29
Creative destruction. Network effects. Disruption patterns.
How industries change, why moats erode, where competitive advantage lives.
ESSAY 20
Creative Destruction
Schumpeter: capitalism destroys to create. S&P 500 turnover is accelerating: 1958 average tenure 61 years; 2024 ~15 years. No moat is permanent. Buy & hold forever = wrong assumption. Reassess moats every 3-5 years.
Moat erosion
ESSAY 21
Network Effects
Metcalfe's Law: value = N². Facebook, Visa, Microsoft Office. Network effects = winner-take-most. Disruption requires switching costs to crash. VN: Zalo dominates via SMS replacement; VietQR dominates via standardization. Identify network monopolies early.
N²
ESSAY 22
Innovator's Dilemma (Christensen)
Big companies can't disrupt themselves — disruption comes from below at lower margins. Listening to the wrong customer = death. Kodak, Blockbuster, Nokia. Apply: an incumbent dominant in one segment is fragile to a lower-tier entrant moving up-market.
Disruption from below
ESSAY 23
Strategic Power Sources
Hamilton Helmer's 7 Powers: scale economies, network, counter-positioning, switching costs, brand, cornered resource, process power. A real moat = 1+ Power, not just "great brand". Audit your portfolio: which Power does each holding have?
7 Powers
ESSAY 24
Business Cycle Phases
4 phases: early (recovery, low capacity), mid (growth, full capacity), late (overheating, leverage), recession. Sector rotation by phase: early = cyclicals, mid = growth, late = defensives, recession = quality survivors. VN Q4/2024 = late phase signals.
4 phases
ESSAY 25
Diffusion of Innovation
S-curve adoption: innovators 2.5%, early adopters 13.5%, early majority 34%, late majority 34%, laggards 16%. Investment timing: enter at the chasm crossing (15-20% adoption) — risk lower, upside still 5-10x. EV in VN ~5% — pre-chasm.
S-curve timing
ESSAY 26
Increasing Returns
Industrial economics: diminishing returns. Information economics: increasing returns (more users → more value → more users). Software, platforms, marketplaces. Different valuation framework needed — DCF doesn't capture network compounding.
Increasing returns
ESSAY 27
Long Tail
Anderson 2004: digital reduces inventory cost → niches viable. Aggregate of niches = 50%+ of market. Amazon books, Spotify songs, Netflix shows. Investment: aggregators of long tail (platforms) are more durable than the head (a single hit).
Long tail
ESSAY 28
Power Laws (Pareto)
Distributions are non-Gaussian: 80/20 rule everywhere. Top 1% companies generate 80% of market value. VN: top 30 stocks ~80% of market cap. Implication: indexes are concentrated → diversification myth. Active selection of top 30 = a different game.
Power law
ESSAY 29
Mean Reversion vs Persistence
Financial returns mean-revert (eventually). Operational performance can persist with moats. Visa ROIC 30% for 20 years — persistence. Generic retailer 10% → 8% → 6% — reversion. Knowing which framework applies when = critical edge.
Reversion vs persistence
PART 4 · Science & Complexity — Essays 30-38
Phase transitions. Iceberg metaphor. Fitness landscape. Adaptation.
Markets behave like complex physical systems — not equilibrium.
ESSAY 30
Phase Transitions
Water → ice at 0°C. Markets behave physically — quiet → quiet → quiet → SUDDEN. Linear extrapolation fails at phase boundaries. 2008 looked stable until it wasn't. 2020 COVID was similar. Apply: stop assuming smooth — prepare for discontinuity.
Discontinuity
ESSAY 31
Iceberg Metaphor
10% visible above water — 90% below. Market price = surface; underlying credit, leverage, sentiment = the iceberg below. 2007 mortgage AAA looked safe — leverage 30:1 below the water. Read what's NOT visible.
Below water
ESSAY 32
Fitness Landscape
Biology: species adapt to local fitness peaks. Stuck on a local peak = can't reach a higher peak across the valley. Companies are the same — local optimum trap. Kodak film at peak → couldn't cross to the digital valley. Apply: an incumbent that's stuck = fragile to a nimble newcomer.
Local peak trap
ESSAY 33
Adaptation Failure
Why do smart organizations fail to adapt? Success makes you inflexible. Past success = current process = future failure. GE jet engines, Sears retail, Boeing 737-MAX. Apply: avoid "best-in-class" overpriced — past success = lagging indicator.
Past success trap
ESSAY 34
Stocks & Bonds Long-term
Siegel data 200 years: stocks 6.5% real, bonds 3.5% real, cash 0.5% real. Stocks dominate over 30+ years — but with -50% drawdowns. Asset allocation = optimize utility, not return. Most retail need 60/40 not 100/0.
200-year view
ESSAY 35
Risk-Reward in Practice
Theoretical Sharpe ratio assumes Gaussian. Real distributions are fat-tailed. LTCM blew up because of a Gaussian model. Apply: stress test with 4-5 sigma, not 2 sigma. A -25% scenario should fit your budget — not just -10%.
Fat tail stress
ESSAY 36
Volatility & Information
Volatility ≠ noise — sometimes it's information. A spike in volatility = regime change signal. VIX >30 historically marks fear extreme = buying opportunity (probabilistic). Don't ignore vol — read it.
Vol as signal
ESSAY 37
Diversification Limits
10-15 stocks already give 80% of diversification benefit. 30+ stocks = closet indexing with cost. Concentrated 8-12 best ideas = optimal balance. Mauboussin: focus + sizing = where edge lives. Over-diversification kills returns.
Concentrated focus
ESSAY 38
Pulling It All Together
Synthesis: "Investing is not a science but a craft. Multiple lenses, accumulated wisdom, process discipline." 38 essays don't collapse to 1 rule — they're a toolkit. Apply the lens that fits the situation. Multidisciplinary breadth = real edge.
Multi-lens toolkit
